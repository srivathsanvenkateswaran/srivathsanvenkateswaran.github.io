How this was worked out

  • Timings come from the Indian Railways timetable in force (via erail.in) for the exact date of journey. They are scheduled times; on the day, late running moves crossings by the same amount.
  • A crossing is the moment the two trains pass each other. Between halts the position is interpolated, so the place is "between A and B"; when it lands inside a halt it says so.
  • Overtakes are shown from the timetable too. In practice an overtake happens at the station where the slower train is held, so treat the place as "near" rather than exact.
  • Suburban EMUs around Chennai and Delhi are left out, since they run on the separate suburban tracks. Goods trains have no public timetable and are not included.
  • Trains that join the route at a junction without a halt there are tracked from the junction; trains added after this page was built (last-minute specials) will not appear.
